============================
Astrid's Guide to Vampirism
============================

So, the curse took you after all.

My name is Astrid, and while my other guide covers the raising of the dead, this one is for the risen. Astrid's Guide to Vampirism appears in your inventory the moment you create a new character - and again, if you somehow don't already have it, the instant any transformation completes. Read it for the full account; the summary is below.

============================
Installation instructions
============================
Download the mod for your operating system, and unzip it into your StreamingAssets folder.

============================
Main features
============================

Reading the Book Before You've Turned
The book does more than tell Astrid's story if you're not a vampire yet. Below character level 2, reading it just tells you to come back once you've grown - no offer, nothing else happens. At level 2 and above, it offers you the curse outright: a Yes/No prompt in Astrid's own voice. Refuse, and nothing changes. Accept, and the transformation begins immediately and for real - the same day-1 dream, day-3 false death, and two-week jump to a fresh start as a full vampire that a wild bite would have given you, with no way to talk your way out of it once you've said yes. An already-turned vampire reading the book instead learns any new spells it holds, exactly as before.

Loitering Without a Full Belly
Rest For A While and Rest Until Healed only lock up once your blood is fully spent - 0 Blood Points specifically. Above that, even mid-bloodlust, you can still open the Rest window, Loiter, and fully rest.

Daylight Weakens You, Hooded or Not
Step outside in daylight and your vampiric attribute/skill bonuses vanish (see Humanity below for exactly how much) and your maximum Health is halved, whether or not you're hooded. Raising your hood uses DFU's own built-in cloak variant cycling - middle-click your equipped cloak on the paper doll, an accessory slot, or in your inventory list to cycle it to its hooded-up appearance - and stops the burning outright, including the "Damage from Sunlight" character disadvantage if you took it, and lets you fast-travel by day. It does not undo the weakness - you're still diminished, just not dying to it. Lose the cloak and the hood falls with it. At Humanity 10 you don't need the cloak at all - see below.

Blood Points
Your real hunger dial - a 0-10 pool, starting full the moment you turn. A persistent HUD tracker (ten dots, filled for each point you have - see the Mod Settings entries below to reposition or hide it) shows it at a glance; check the exact number and a one-word label any time alongside Humanity by pressing Status ("I" by default). Feeding refills it; spells, thralls, and healing spend it; and it drains passively on its own, 1 point every 12 in-game hours, whether you spend anything or not - full to empty by time alone is 5 days, so spending, not waiting, is the real pressure. 0 points is "starving": it's what locks Rest, what Frenzy Events check for, and what a successful feed's forced-kill risk keys off once you're down to 2 or fewer.

Sneak-Feeding
A separate keybind (configurable in Mod Settings) opens a Feed encounter on whatever you're looking at. On an awake townsperson, this is a short Narrative Feeding Scene (see below) - a flat Charm/Stalk/Feast menu only ever appears as an automatic, defensive fallback if a scene genuinely can't be assembled (no live vampirism effect found, or the scene data failed to load), never as something you choose. That flat menu offers three options indoors in a home or inn/tavern, two anywhere else:
- Charm (Etiquette, rolled twice, taking the better result) - never calls the guards, win or lose, indoors or outdoors. Grants +3 Blood Points, Humanity-neutral.
- Stalk (Stealth) - indoors only. Success is quiet (same +3 blood as Charm); failure calls the guards.
- Feast (Hand-to-Hand) - outdoors, guards are called unconditionally regardless of outcome (in a Narrative Feeding Scene, see below, a successful Feast on the streets at night draws no guards instead - the streets are simply empty - though a failed Feast, or any Feast at the Palace, still always calls them). Success kills the victim outright and refills your blood to full, instead of the usual +3 feed, and costs 1 Humanity; a failed indoor Feast no longer calls the guards on its own - see "The Victim Fights Back" below for what happens instead.
On a corpse or a passive (non-hostile) monster or enemy, anywhere, feeding is instant - no menu, no roll, no location requirement, since neither can wake up and cry out (each corpse can only be drained once). A human townsperson's corpse grants +3 blood and costs 1 Humanity, same as it would alive. Every OTHER corpse - any wild monster or enemy you've killed - scales with how tough it was: Max(1, its maximum Health / the "Corpse Blood Divisor" Mod Setting, default 40), so a rat gives a trickle and an ogre gives a flood. Undead and golem/atronach corpses (skeletons, zombies, ghosts, liches, iron/fire/ice/flesh atronachs) have nothing to give at all - "Nothing flows in this one" - though a rival vampire's corpse is never treated as bloodless, see below. Feeding on a LIVE passive animal or non-hostile creature, rather than draining its corpse, grants a flat +1, Humanity-neutral - restraint on something still breathing, versus draining a corpse to the last drop. If your blood is already at 2 points or fewer when a non-lethal approach succeeds, your live Willpower is your chance to resist the bloodlust - fail it, and the victim dies just like a Feast kill would (a forced kill still only grants the normal +3, but costs 1 Humanity same as any other killing; Feast itself always kills on success regardless of blood level, and corpses/passive monsters are never at risk). Any kill leaves a corpse behind, with gold matching vanilla's own pickpocket-a-townsperson odds. Every successful feed plays a sound, briefly flashes the screen red, reports your current Blood Points in the HUD message, and heals you for 10% of your max Health and Fatigue - 30% for a Feast kill, matching the greater risk. After every feed attempt, a follow-up message describes what happened, varying by method, location, and outcome.

You can't attempt to feed on anyone or anything while guards are actively pursuing you - settle that first. Each individual townsperson can only be fed on once every 24 hours, win or lose; on cooldown, a fresh encounter just tells you they've had enough of you for one day, with no cost and no new cooldown stamped.

Narrative Feeding Scenes
Approaching an awake townsperson through Sneak-Feeding opens a short, procedurally-assembled scene instead of a flat Charm/Stalk/Feast menu - same underlying rolls, blood, Humanity, and guard rules underneath, just told as a mini encounter with real stakes. This is the core feeding experience, not an optional mode; it automatically falls back to the classic flat menu only if it genuinely can't find what it needs to build a scene, so feeding never simply breaks. If you're at 3 blood or below, the scene opens with a one-time Willpower check ("the hunger is louder than your intent tonight") - fail it and the whole encounter turns bloodlust-driven, meaning even a gentle success at the end will still turn into a full lethal Feast. The opening itself is chosen for your exact situation (location, indoors/outdoors, time of night, even a rare Dark Brotherhood-flavored "sanctioned feeding" opening where every non-lethal approach auto-succeeds) and always presents four distinct, freshly-written approaches plus a free Withdraw that costs nothing and starts no cooldown - never the same fixed Charm/Stalk/Feast trio twice, though exactly one approach is always the lethal, take-them-by-force option. Roughly half the time (adjustable via "Scene Complication Chance") a mid-scene complication interrupts with its own twist and its own small choice - anything from harmless flavor to a serious escalation. An escalated encounter can be talked back down with a Thaumaturgy check; fail that, and it guarantees the same fights-back combat a botched Feast can trigger (see "The Victim Fights Back" below). Sometimes your mark isn't human at all: a vampire reveal drops straight into a fight with no way to talk it down; a werewolf or wereboar wearing human form CAN still be talked down with a Thaumaturgy check; a Daedra in disguise, like a vampire, allows no de-escalation at all. Rarer still, a hunter can catch you mid-scene - your actual victim vanishes (they resurface roughly a day later) and a hostile Knight or Mage takes their place, trained and immune to Mesmerize's gaze. In a Temple, an extremely dark culmination is possible: every NPC present is killed in one stroke, your blood fills completely, Humanity drops by 3, and no guards are ever called - there's no one left to raise the alarm. Choices are presented on full-width, word-wrapped parchment-style cards you click to select, rather than a cramped single-line list that truncates long option text.

Feed Scene Music
The moment a Narrative Feeding Scene begins, the game's own music fades out over a couple of seconds while a dedicated heartbeat track fades in and loops for as long as the scene lasts; however the scene ends - the resolution box closing, an immediate Withdraw, an aborted complication, or any reveal/hunter-arrival/temple-massacre handoff into real combat, Escape included - the heartbeat fades back out and your normal music resumes automatically. If the separately-installed Dynamic Music mod is active, it's detected automatically and handled the same way, so the swap works cleanly with either music system. A Mod Settings toggle ("Feed Scene Music," on by default, in the Feeding section) turns the whole swap off - your normal music just keeps playing through scenes untouched.

Feed Scene Voice Acting
Each Narrative Feeding Scene message can play its own recorded voice line, matched to that message's own text. A Mod Settings toggle ("Feed Scene Voice Acting," on by default, in the Feeding section) turns this off entirely if you'd rather read scenes silently; any line already playing when you toggle it off stops immediately.

Mesmerize Command Menu
Cast Mesmerize (see its own entry below) and your gaze goes to work. The very next townsperson you activate - not fed on, just looked in the eye and talked to, the same button you'd use to greet anyone - opens a command menu instead of ordinary dialogue, with a prompt noting their will is now yours and asking what you do with them next; that primed gaze lasts a few in-game minutes and is spent the instant it lands on someone, win or lose nothing further, so use it or lose it. The same cast also pacifies every hostile human mind within ten feet of you - activate any of THEM afterward and the same menu opens on them instead. The options differ by target:
- Feed (pacified enemies only, not townspeople - Charm/Stalk already cover feeding on a townsperson) - guaranteed, no roll, once per target. The usual feed effects for a living target (+3 blood for a human-class enemy, +1 for an animal/monster-class one), no extra cost beyond what Mesmerize itself already charged to get here.
- Loot - opens their belongings without killing them. A townsperson yields the same odds as a kill would (33% chance of 1-6 gold, otherwise nothing); a mesmerized enemy hands over their real equipment/inventory outright, leaving them alive but empty-handed. Each target can only be looted once per encounter.
- Interrogate - a townsperson answers every question fully and cooperatively (with a distinctly compelled, dreamlike quality to how they speak), no "doesn't know" brush-offs. A pacified enemy encountered in a dungeon, if another quest-relevant target is currently placed somewhere in that same dungeon, instead breaks and reveals the compass direction (and up/down, if it's on a different level) toward that other target - otherwise this option doesn't appear at all.
A pacified enemy stays reachable across as many activations as you like, but Feed and Loot's own gold roll are each one-shot against any single target - Feed once, then come back for Loot or Interrogate instead. Create Thrall is no longer offered here at all - see Post-Feed Offers below for the only way to gain a thrall now.

The Victim Fights Back
Sometimes a target isn't the easy mark they looked like. A failed Charm or Stalk feed has a chance of this - (10 minus your Humanity) times 2.5 percent, so up to 25% at Humanity 0 and never at Humanity 10 - while a failed Feast attempt triggers it every time, no roll of its own needed. When it happens, the townsperson vanishes and a hostile enemy takes their place: 90% of the time an ordinary Thief, Knight, or Mage who was tougher than they looked (they eventually return, roughly a day later); 8% of the time an actual rival vampire; 2% of the time something far older, a Vampire Ancient wearing a mortal face. A vanished vampire or ancient never comes back - they were never really a townsperson at all. A successful Charm or Stalk feed can also, at a flat 1% chance, reveal a vampire or ancient after the fact - you fed, and the blood was wrong. Mesmerize's own AoE pacify has no hold over the vampire/ancient variants at all - only living, human minds calm at its touch.

Post-Feed Offers
The only way left to gain a thrall or a created vampire - Create Thrall no longer appears in any menu at all. After a Narrative Feeding Scene resolves as a successful, non-violent feed (Stealth/Charm/Opportunity - never a Feast, never a bloodlust-converted one, never an aborted or withdrawn scene), there's a small chance of one additional, unprompted message box: your victim is offered up as either a Create Vampire opportunity (rare, 2% by default, checked first) or a Create Thrall one (uncommon, 10% by default, only checked if the vampire offer didn't come up) - at most one offer per scene, and none at all if your shared Legion Budget has no room for the offer that was about to be made (see Combat Allies/Thrall System below). Both rates have their own Mod Settings sliders ("Create Vampire Offer Chance"/"Create Thrall Offer Chance," in the Feeding section) if you'd rather make them rarer, more common, or turn either off entirely (0%). Accepting either costs 3 Blood Points (Desperate Casting HP Cost applies if you're short, and can kill you outright if reckless) and is Humanity-neutral either way; declining costs nothing and leaves no trace - the next clean feed rolls fresh. A Create Thrall acceptance works exactly like the old menu option did; a Create Vampire acceptance turns the victim directly into a Vampire ally on the spot, no waiting, feedable for 6 blood at a time instead of 3 (see Thrall System below) - this offer is the only way left to gain a vampire ally at all; an existing thrall can no longer be upgraded into one afterward.

Frenzy Events
At 0 Blood Points, lingering near townspeople risks the beast taking over. Checked once a game-minute while you're near a valid, feedable townsperson (not guard-pursued, not on their own 24-hour cooldown): chance is (10 minus your Humanity) times 2 percent, so 0% at full Humanity - a fully human vampire's will is entirely their own, even starving - up to 20% at Humanity 0. A triggered frenzy rolls your live Willpower (already carrying the empty-blood penalty) to resist; success is just a close call with a flavor message, nothing else. Failure means an involuntary feed attempt on the nearest valid target, resolved as a Stalk-style feed you didn't choose - quiet success or guards-and-a-fights-back-chance on failure, with the same low-blood forced-kill risk any other feed carries. Once a frenzy actually fires, it won't fire again for 4 in-game hours. A Mod Settings toggle ("Frenzy Events," on by default, in the Feeding section) turns the whole thing off - no checks, no rolls, zero overhead - and can be flipped mid-session without a restart.

Night Walkers
By default (Mod Settings, "Night Walker Count," default 3), a handful of ordinary townspeople keep wandering city streets after dark instead of vanilla's usual empty-streets-at-night behavior - so there's actually someone to hunt after dusk. They're indistinguishable from any other townsperson to Sneak-Feeding or anything else, match the local region's population, and despawn automatically at dawn or the moment you change location - no save-game footprint. Set the count to 0 to disable entirely.

Populate Residences At Night
On by default (Mod Settings toggle). Vanilla hides the people living inside ordinary houses outside each building's fixed "open hours," even though they're technically still there. This mod force-reactivates any hidden residents the moment you step into an unowned house, regardless of the vanilla schedule, so a house that vanilla would show as empty at odd hours still has feedable occupants. Your own owned house(s) are never affected, and shops/guildhalls/temples/taverns are untouched - this only touches plain residences.

Fast Travel Blood Cost
Fast travel now costs Blood Points directly - 1 point per 5 days of travel time, rounded up - in place of (not on top of) the passive drain that would otherwise have ticked during the jumped time. Can't afford it? You still travel, you just arrive at 0 blood. Walking, resting, and waiting still drain blood normally either way.

Wilderness Hunt
Press the Sneak-Feed key with no one to feed on and you're not indoors, in a dungeon, or standing in a real town/hamlet/village - instead of doing nothing, it starts a hunt for animal prey, told as its own short scene just like a townsperson encounter. A lone farm, shrine, or wayside tavern (World of Daggerfall adds plenty of these) doesn't block it - only an actual settlement, a roof over your head, or a dungeon does. A handful of approaches (Mysticism, Stealth, Running, Climbing, depending on the opening) let you track your quarry down, with the same kind of mid-hunt complications a townsperson scene can throw at you - a stranger scent crossing the trail, a river to ford, a stretch of noisy deadfall to cross. Success spawns one hostile animal directly in front of you, weighted toward mid-yield prey (bears and sabertooth tigers alongside rats and bats) - nothing feeds automatically, you fight it and drain the corpse afterward through the normal corpse economy (Vampiric Drain or a hand-drain both work, HP/divisor blood same as any wild kill). Failure ends the hunt empty-handed. Rarely, the trail leads to something that was never prey at all - an Orc, a Spriggan, a Centaur - a harder fight for a richer corpse, not a mugging; werewolves and wereboars never appear this way (that surprise belongs to the reveal system alone). Free to attempt, but capped by a cooldown between attempts (Mod Settings, "Animal Hunt Cooldown Hours," default 12, 0 disables it) - stepping back before committing to an approach is free, but rolling one, win or lose, starts the cooldown fresh; attempting again too soon just tells you the wild has nothing more for you yet. This is the wilderness recovery valve now - Hunt Animal (the old on-demand summon spell) has been removed entirely.

Combat Allies
Every Thrall, created vampire, and Dominated creature draws on one shared Legion Budget - (10 minus your Humanity) times 10, plus your Willpower times 3 - so a beast-leaning, strong-willed vampire commands a far larger court than a nearly-human one can. Thralls and created vampires each cost a flat amount against that budget; a Dominated creature costs more the tougher it is (its own max Health divided by the "Dominate Upkeep Divisor" Mod Setting, default 4 - lower means Dominate can hold tougher creatures for the same budget). Every ally in your court, regardless of kind, actively fights at your side - following you, engaging hostile enemies near you or attacking you, focusing whatever you're currently fighting, and teleporting back to your side if it ever falls behind (stuck on geometry, left behind by fast travel, etc.) rather than standing wherever it was created. An optional Mod Setting ("Allies Ignore Each Other's Collision," off by default) lets a large court pass through each other instead of jostling for space in tight corridors. Summon Court (see New Spells below) calls that same teleport-back behavior on demand for your whole court at once.

Paper Doll Head Option
A Mod Settings toggle lets you keep your normal racial head on the paper doll instead of the vampire head texture.

Hunter's Sight Light Range
A Mod Settings slider (1-100, default 35) controls how far the light attached to you while Hunter's Sight is active reaches.

Hunter's Sight Screen Tint
A Mod Settings toggle (on by default) - disable it to drop the red screen tint Hunter's Sight applies while active; the light itself still works either way.

Hunter's Sight Light Color / Tint Color
Two Mod Settings color pickers controlling the color of the light and the color (including opacity, via the picker's hex field) of the screen tint Hunter's Sight applies - default to a reddish "Night-Eye" look for both, but either can be set to any color independently.

Blood Sense Detection Range
A Mod Settings slider (1-200, default 120) controls how far, in feet, Blood Sense detects living creatures while active.

Blood Tracker Display
Two Mod Settings control the HUD dot tracker: "Show Blood Tracker" (on by default) hides it entirely if you'd rather keep a clean HUD, and "Blood Tracker Position" (default Top Left) picks which screen corner it sits in.

Corpse Blood Divisor
A Mod Settings slider (10-100, default 40) controls how generous draining a wild monster/enemy corpse is - blood gained is Max(1, its maximum Health / this number), so a lower number means more blood per kill.

Desperate Casting HP Cost %
A Mod Settings slider (5-50, default 20) - casting a blood-costing spell or action with insufficient Blood Points no longer refuses; each missing point instead costs this percent of your maximum Health, paid directly and lethal if reckless. A distinct message always tells you when you've paid this way instead of the normal blood cost.

Climates & Calories Compatibility
If Climates & Calories is installed, this mod grants temporary GodMode across the instant two-week time jump and respawn that happens when your transformation completes, so you don't take starvation/exposure damage the moment you regain control.

World of Daggerfall Compatibility
If World of Daggerfall is installed, the farmers and other decorative townspeople outside its farms and shrines can be fed on through the Sneak-Feeding keybind just like an ordinary townsperson - they're plain decorative figures with no dialogue of their own (even vanilla can't talk to them), so Mesmerize's Interrogate option has nothing to draw out of one beyond its opening flavor line. If you find a World of Daggerfall person the feed key still doesn't recognize, check Player.log for a "SneakFeed: raycast hit 'DaggerfallBillboard [TEXTURE.X, Index=Y]'... none of StaticNPC/..." line and report the archive number (X).

Blood-Linked Attributes
Willpower and Personality both track your current Blood Points directly: Willpower drops by 1 for every point below full (0 at 10 blood, -10 at 0), while Personality swings from +10 at full blood down to -20 empty - friendlier fresh off a feed, more ill-tempered the thinner your blood runs.

Thrall System
Create Thrall is reached only through a Post-Feed Offer now (see above) - there's no menu option, no roll, and no way to seek it out on demand. When the rare offer appears and you say yes, it always succeeds: 3 Blood Points spent (giving your own blood makes you hungrier), and the victim becomes a permanent ally, following and fighting for you, using the same shared Legion Budget every ally in your court draws from (see Combat Allies below). Short on blood? It's no longer a refusal - the shortfall is paid in Health instead (see "Desperate Casting HP Cost %" above). The only outright refusal is the budget having no room for another thrall already, which suppresses the offer from appearing at all rather than letting you decline something you couldn't have anyway. A created vampire (see below) costs more against that same budget than an ordinary thrall does. If the budget ever shrinks below what your court is currently spending (only a rising Humanity can do this - Willpower only ever adds room), your oldest ally - thrall, created vampire, or anything currently under Dominate, whichever is actually oldest - breaks free and turns hostile on the spot, repeating until everything fits again; reaching Humanity 10 frees your very last one with its own dedicated message (fully human holds no one in bondage). A freed thrall can be killed normally, or spared with Mesmerize (freed created vampires can't be Mesmerized - see Mesmerize's own entry below).

Aim at one of your own thralls and press the feed key for a separate "Mesmerized" menu: Feed, Feast, Cancel. There's no Create Vampire option here - an existing thrall can no longer be upgraded into a vampire ally after the fact; the only door to a vampire ally at all is the rare Post-Feed Offer above.
- Feed - once per 24 hours per thrall. Grants +3 Blood Points (+6 for a created vampire) with the usual heal, but risks the bond: a d100-above-Humanity roll (same shape as Dominate's own success chance) decides whether it stays bound afterward. You get the feed either way - the roll only decides whether it turns on you afterward.
- Feast - kills your own thrall outright: full blood refill, 30% heal, the normal -1 Humanity murder penalty. No special mercy for it being yours.

Thralls are tracked permanently and re-appear at your side after scene transitions, fast travel, and save/reload - not literally the same physical body carried across every transition, but re-summoned near you each time from your own persistent roster, so the roster survives everything including a full quit and relaunch.

Vampire Clans
The moment any transformation completes - reading the book or a wild bite, no distinction - you wake beneath the stone to this mod's own words in place of the vanilla message, and choose which of the nine vampire clans your blood belongs to: a paused, alphabetical list, with each clan's gift spelled out for confirmation before you commit (or go back and read another), the heartbeat of your new blood underneath it all. Each clan carries its own passive or spell modification, replacing vanilla's old per-clan spell grants entirely:
- Anthotis - +20 Intelligence (scales with Humanity/daylight the same way every other clan bonus does).
- Garlythi - Blood Surge also grants Shield, at the same real magnitude as vanilla's own Shield spell, for Blood Surge's own duration.
- Haarvenu - grants Vampiric Ice (see New Spells below), Vampiric Flames' equal in ice instead of fire.
- Khulari - while Blood Surge is active, your weapon hits have a 10% chance to Paralyze.
- Lyrezi - Mist Form also grants Invisibility for its duration.
- Montalion - +10 Willpower (on top of the universal vampire Willpower bonus, same scaling as every other clan bonus).
- Selenu - Blood Surge also grants Fire Immunity for its duration.
- Thrafey - Quicken Blood's Health/Fatigue return is 10% stronger.
- Vraseth - Blood Surge also raises your Running skill by the same amount as its weapon-skill bonus.

New Spells (all granted together, unconditionally, the moment your transformation completes and you've picked a clan - no need to read the book first, no level gate; potency scales with Humanity instead, see each entry - every spell costs at least 1 Blood Point except Vampiric Drain and Devour Thrall, both of which are how you ACQUIRE blood rather than spend it. Every spell's Magicka cost is charged then immediately refunded - Magicka is just the toll the engine insists on collecting, Blood is the real cost - though you still need to have it on hand at the moment of casting. Running short of blood no longer refuses a cast outright: see "Desperate Casting HP Cost %" above, a missing point costs Health instead, with its own distinct message so you always know which currency you just spent. This is now the ENTIRE vampire spellset - vanilla's own Levitate, Charm Mortal, and Calm Humanoid grants are stripped the moment the curse takes hold, since Mist Form and Mesmerize already cover the same ground. Reading the book still grants the same spells too - purely a backup at that point, useful for getting one back if you ever deliberately delete it from your spellbook)
Vampiric Drain - a self-target spell, fully free (no Magicka, no blood): senses every corpse within 10 feet and feeds off all of them at once, scaled the same way a manual corpse drain is - Max(1, maximum Health / the Corpse Blood Divisor) per wild corpse, a flat +3 (and -1 Humanity) for a murdered townsperson caught in the pile, a full refill if a rival vampire's corpse is among them, and a silent skip for anything bloodless - with a red-tinted impact flash over each corpse actually drained. Each corpse can only be drained this way once, and a corpse already fed on normally (or vice versa) doesn't count again.
Blood Sense (formerly Scent of Prey) - a toggle, not a timed buff: cast once to turn on, cast again to turn off. While active, every living thing within 120 feet (people, animals, hostile enemies alike, but not the undead or daedra) trails a drifting red mist you can see through walls, refreshed continuously - anything that's currently the target of an active quest burns gold instead of red, so you always know when the blood of destiny is nearby. No Humanity scaling; costs 1 Blood Point to raise (once, not per round).
Mesmerize - a self-cast now, not aimed at anything. One cast, two effects at once: it sharpens your own gaze (the next townsperson you activate, however long that takes within the next few in-game minutes, opens the Mesmerize Command Menu instead of ordinary talk - see above), and it calms every hostile human mind within ten feet of you on the spot (the same effect as vanilla's own Charm Mortal and Calm Humanoid together, rolled individually per enemy), at a cost of 1 Blood Point per cast regardless of how many minds it touches. Reliability of the AoE calm scales with Humanity - always succeeds at 0, as low as a coin flip at 10, stronger the more beast you are, same as every other Humanity-scaled vampire working; the gaze itself never rolls, it simply primes. Has no hold over another vampire, the undead, daedra, or a lycanthrope at all - only living, human minds are vulnerable, and a trained vampire hunter's own preparation resists it regardless. Activate a pacified enemy afterward to open the same command menu on them - Feed, Loot, and (in a dungeon, if another quest target is placed there too) the directional Interrogate all do.
Vampiric Flames - an area-of-effect Destruction spell, 1 Blood Point per cast, charged the moment the spell is cast - even if the blast lands on empty ground and hits nobody. Damage is a flat 50 base plus (10 minus your Humanity) for every character level - weaker the more human you remain, stronger the more beast, and now grows as you level up too.
Vampiric Ice - clan Haarvenu's own working, granted only to a Haarvenu vampire. Identical to Vampiric Flames in every respect - same cost, same damage formula - just ice instead of fire.
Suppress Beast - for 24 hours, you forgo your clan bonus even at night, gaining 1 Humanity for the discipline. Costs 1 Blood Point, charged only on a genuine cast - a cast still on the previous cooldown costs nothing. Cannot be recast until a full day has passed since the last successful cast.
Dominate - bends a living creature (anything but the undead or daedra) to fight at your side, at a cost of 1 Blood Point per attempt, charged the moment the spell is cast, win or lose the roll. Chance to succeed is (10 minus your Humanity) times 10 - a beast-leaning vampire succeeds more often. Duration runs the other way: 20 rounds, plus 4 more for every point of Humanity you still hold - the more human you remain, the longer a successful hold actually lasts, even though it lands less often. A Mod Settings toggle ("Permanent Dominate," off by default) removes the duration limit entirely - a Dominated creature bound this way never reverts to hostile on its own. Concurrent cap is the same shared Legion Budget the Thrall System uses (see Combat Allies above), not a fixed number - a Dominated creature's own upkeep cost against it scales with how tough the creature is, and applies regardless of the Permanent Dominate setting: the creature reverts to hostile, unharmed, when the duration ends (if not permanent), or earlier if the budget can no longer hold your whole court and it happens to be the oldest member.
Hunter's Sight - a toggle, not a timed buff: cast once to turn on, cast again to turn off. While active, a light travels with you to help you see in the dark, plus (unless disabled) a tint across the whole screen - see the Hunter's Sight settings above for range and color. No Humanity scaling; costs 1 Blood Point to raise.
Summon Court - an instant, free, self-target spell that teleports your whole court - every thrall, every created vampire, and anything currently under Dominate - to your side at once. Existing Dominate durations aren't refreshed by this, only repositioned. Fizzles with a message if you have no one to call.
Devour Thrall - a single-target spell that instantly kills one of your own allies (a thrall, a created vampire, or anything currently bound by Dominate) and drains it exactly as feeding on it would - the same +3/+6 a normal thrall/vampire feed already gives, or (for a Dominated creature, which has no fixed amount of its own) the same max-HP/Corpse-Blood-Divisor scaling an ordinary wild corpse drain uses. Free of any blood cost, same as feeding on a thrall already is. Their corpse gives nothing further afterward - already drained, same as any other corpse you've fed on.
Mist Form - a toggle, not a timed buff: cast once (1 Blood Point) to turn on, cast again to turn off. While active you levitate - faster the lower your Humanity, up to double speed at Humanity 0 - and take no damage from any weapon that isn't magical. Doors inside any interior, dungeon or building alike, are simply no obstacle at all to you personally, walked through without even needing to open them (everyone else, enemies included, still has to open or break them as normal); exterior town/building entrances still need the normal activate key, but open as if unlocked the instant you do, no lock strength or level too high to matter. Enemies are no obstacle either - you drift straight through them, not just their doors. You cannot attack or cast any other spell while unmade like this - only Mist Form's own recast still works, to knit back together. Costs another Blood Point every 4 rounds it's active; running out of blood no longer collapses the form - the drain switches to Health instead, at the same four-round rhythm, with its own message so you know the mist is feeding on you now.
Quicken Blood - a self-target instant heal: 1 Blood Point for 25% of your maximum Health and Fatigue. Cast again for more. At 0 blood it inverts instead of refusing: Health itself pays the cost, and Fatigue is what's restored - your own strength steadying you when there's no blood left to spend.
Blood Surge - a self-target combat buff, 10 rounds: a flat bonus to every weapon skill and Hand-to-Hand, stronger the more beast you are (+10 at Humanity 6-10, +20 at 1-5, +30 at 0). Costs 1 Blood Point. Recasting refreshes the duration rather than stacking, and re-evaluates the bonus if your Humanity has changed since. Garlythi, Selenu, Khulari, and Vraseth each get an extra effect while it's active - see Vampire Clans above.

============================
Compatibility
============================

Astrid's Guide to Necromancy
Both mods currently register their own book's read-handler on the same shared "any book" slot (Books.Book0), and only one delegate can occupy that slot at a time. This mod's own registration chains to whatever was already registered there, so it stays compatible regardless of load order - but Necromancy's own handler does not chain back, so if it loads after this mod its book may stop granting spells on read (it will still open and show its text either way). Load this mod after Astrid's Guide to Necromancy if running both.
